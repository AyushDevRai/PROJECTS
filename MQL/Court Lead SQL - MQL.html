From MQL to installed court

Every MQL follows one sales cycle. Dealers close every sale, big or small; the sales team qualifies the lead, picks the dealer and hand-holds the deal through each stage. The grid cell decides how fast and how often we call. Stages marked draft come from the pipeline draft; added stages fill gaps a court project needs. The goal for every lead is a handed-over court that earns a referral, not just a closed order.

Expected timeline

How long each grade should take from MQL to a handed-over court, built from the playbook and stage SLA settings. These are starting estimates. The table below compares them with real dates as leads move, so you can adjust the settings once there's enough data.

Monsoon calendar

Outdoor acrylic can't be applied in sustained rain, so installs wait out each region's monsoon. The cut-off dates show when an MQL must arrive to finish before the rains. Plan regional campaigns ahead of them. Deals that close after the cut-off become a monsoon booking: PO and advance now, install slot after the rains, with keep-warm calls until then. Edit the windows in Scoring settings.

Expected vs actual

Each phase is measured from stage dates on real MQLs. A phase needs 5 leads before it shows a status; at 10 or more, a gap over 25% suggests a new setting value.
